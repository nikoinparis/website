Who I Am

I'm a Data Science major at UC Berkeley in the Applied Mathematics & Modeling concentration, with a Statistics minor. My coursework spans probability, time series, stochastic processes, statistical inference, numerical analysis, and optimization.

I spent two years as a Research Data Analyst at Berkeley Haas, where I ran longitudinal time-series analysis on U.S. News, Financial Times, and Poets & Quants ranking data, built automated Python pipelines against the Google Scholar and Scopus APIs, and produced executive-facing forecasts and dashboards for the Dean's office and board meetings.

I also worked as a Business Analytics & Revenue Optimization Intern at Kebun Wisata Pasirmukti in Indonesia, building demand-forecasting and pricing models from booking data to guide staffing, pricing, and promotional decisions.

Outside of work, I built Atlas Allocation, a regime-aware ETF portfolio research platform combining alpha signals, market-state logic, and benchmark-aware backtesting. The production strategy improved Sharpe from 0.884 to 0.948 and cut max drawdown from -13.98% to -11.60%, with a 2.179 holdout Sharpe.

I'm interested in quant, finance, and data-driven roles more broadly — anywhere I can bring rigorous statistical thinking and research-oriented programming to real decisions, whether that's investment research, corporate finance, or applied data science.

Selected Work

01 /
Atlas Allocation — ETF Portfolio Research & Optimization System

Built a systematic, regime-aware ETF allocation research platform combining alpha signals, market-state logic, checkpointed allocators, and benchmark-aware backtesting. Researched 7 frontier phases spanning state quality, trend strength, re-risking, leadership/crowding, allocator objectives, ML diagnostics, and cross-asset lead-lag behavior. Promoted a Phase 5 fragility-guard strategy to production, improving Sharpe 0.884 → 0.948 and max drawdown -13.98% → -11.60%, with a 2.179 holdout Sharpe.
